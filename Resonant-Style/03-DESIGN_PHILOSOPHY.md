# Resonant Design System
## 03 — DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

## Purpose

The philosophy defines why Resonant should feel calm, personal, and editorial rather than like a generic streaming dashboard.

## Core idea

> Listen with context.

## Principles

### Music first

Music is the reason the interface exists. Give artwork, title, artist, and the act of listening the strongest visual priority. Data should explain the relationship to music, never displace it.

### Keep the thread

Moving from a song to an artist, playlist, taste signal, or recommendation should preserve context. The person should understand where they came from and why the next surface is relevant.

### Show the reasoning

Taste profiles, review findings, and recommendations are observations and estimates. Explain source, freshness, confidence, and the basis of a suggestion. Never present a heuristic as an absolute truth.

### Leave room for surprise

Discovery should have levels of familiarity: Safe picks, Explore, and Wildcard. Surprise is valuable only when it is framed, dismissible, and still connected to the listener’s taste.

### Calm complexity

Resonant can expose rich relationships without showing every control at once. Reveal depth through progressive disclosure, clear headings, and short explanations.

### Personal, not performative

The product is about a person’s relationship with music, not public status. Avoid gamification, engagement pressure, popularity-first hierarchy, and social comparison.

### Honest intelligence

AI and statistical systems should assist rather than impersonate certainty. The user can correct, reject, keep, remove, or mark a recommendation unsure.

## Decision test

1. Does this help the person listen, find, organize, understand, or discover?
2. Does it preserve the thread from the previous surface?
3. Is the reasoning and data freshness understandable?
4. Does the visual hierarchy keep music ahead of metadata?
5. Can the person correct or undo the result?

## Visual direction

Dark, warm, spacious, and editorial. Use strong typographic scale, restrained colour, tactile surfaces, thin rules, and generous negative space. Avoid cold neon, dashboard density, novelty gradients, and decorative charts without a user question.
