# Resonant Design System
## ART DIRECTION

## Purpose

`VISUAL_LANGUAGE.md` defines the visual grammar of Resonant. This chapter turns that grammar into repeatable art direction for pages, renders, photography, artwork, mockups, and marketing material.

Art direction answers a practical question:

> If two different people make two different Resonant visuals, what makes them feel like the same world?

This is a reference system, not a gallery of decoration. Every choice must communicate music, memory, listening, context, taste, connection, or resonance.

## Direction in one sentence

Make one musical idea feel present, give it the context needed to understand it, and leave enough quiet for the listener to stay with it.

## Non-negotiable hierarchy

```text
music object → human context → product explanation → atmosphere
```

If a visual reverses this order, reduce the atmosphere first. If the product explanation competes with the music object, simplify the explanation before shrinking the artwork.

## Status labels

Use these labels when preparing assets and mockups:

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Current** | Verified product behaviour or an approved existing asset. |
| **Fixture** | Clearly labelled sample content used to demonstrate composition. |
| **Concept** | A visual direction that does not claim shipped behaviour. |
| **Moodboard** | A reference used to discuss quality, material, light, or composition. |
| **TODO** | Requires product, legal, licensing, or visual approval. |

Never let a concept render look like a current screenshot by omitting its status.

## Hero styles

The following ten hero styles form a controlled menu. Choose one based on the story; do not combine several hero ideas in one opening.

| # | Hero style | Composition | Best use | Avoid |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01 | **Quiet field** | One dark field, one sentence, one small signal. | Philosophy, opening statement, listening principle. | Adding UI just to fill space. |
| 02 | **Nocturnal observation** | One cinematic photograph with generous copy-safe darkness. | Website opening, chapter transition, product atmosphere. | Generic night photography or overlaid paragraphs. |
| 03 | **Signal macro** | A cropped line, point, or wave enlarged beyond literal scale. | Visual language, motion, analytics, context. | Pretending the crop is a literal audio waveform. |
| 04 | **Artwork anchor** | One album-art fixture with a clear relationship line or caption. | Music, Library, Player, editorial marketing. | Using artwork as a wallpaper behind unrelated UI. |
| 05 | **Single component** | One large product component with one annotated decision. | Player, taste signal, playlist review, connection. | Showing an entire dashboard at thumbnail scale. |
| 06 | **Relationship map** | Two to five nodes connected by intentional paths. | Context, account boundary, discovery, methodology. | Dense networks or unexplained lines. |
| 07 | **Material study** | Paper, screen, or physical surface with one mark and one caption. | Print, logo application, production, texture. | Luxury product photography or material without meaning. |
| 08 | **Typographic pause** | One approved statement at display scale with a quiet field. | Brand, writing, product promise, section opener. | Turning every page into a slogan. |
| 09 | **Layered memory** | Two or three offset traces or frames with one clear origin. | History, taste, archive, playlist return. | Retro filters or sentimental props. |
| 10 | **Threshold** | A crop or doorway from one state into another. | Account, connection, onboarding, discovery transition. | Hiding the next action or implying an irreversible jump. |

### Hero approval test

A hero is ready when:

- it can be described in one sentence;
- the focal point is obvious at thumbnail size;
- the artwork still works with the logo removed;
- the copy has room to breathe;
- the visual does not imply unsupported product functionality;
- the next chapter or action is clearer after seeing it.

## Product renders

Product renders are proof, not wallpaper. The purpose is to show how Resonant’s principles become a usable surface.

### Render recipe

```text
one surface + one question + one focal state + one annotation
```

1. Choose the surface from the verified product or label it as a future concept.
2. State the user question before placing the frame: “What do I want to understand?” or “What can I decide next?”
3. Make one state dominant: connected, empty, reviewing, discovering, or playing.
4. Add only the annotation needed to explain the design decision.
5. Keep the rest of the frame quiet.

### Render formats

| Format | Use | Direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full browser | Establish product context and shell. | Show route, sidebar, Player, and one main surface. |
| Focus crop | Explain one component or decision. | Crop close enough to read hierarchy and state. |
| Device frame | Discuss responsive behaviour. | Use only when the frame clarifies a breakpoint or interaction. |
| Split comparison | Show before/after, current/planned, or familiar/adjacent. | Label both sides and keep the comparison honest. |
| Annotated plate | Explain anatomy or trust boundary. | Use numbered callouts with a short legend. |
| Material mockup | Show physical or marketing use. | Preserve the exact logo asset and disclose the application as concept or proof. |

### Product render rules

- Use the approved logo asset; never redraw it inside a render.
- Use fixture data only when it is labelled and non-identifying.
- Keep artwork, title, artist, and listening state ahead of secondary metadata.
- Do not show unsupported playback, AI, mobile, download, or discovery behaviour as shipped.
- Do not create a feature by adding a decorative card.
- Keep the background simpler than the product surface.
- Use callouts to explain a decision, not to narrate every visible element.

## Background compositions

Backgrounds establish distance and atmosphere. They should not compete with the product or become a second brand.

### Approved background families

| Family | Ingredients | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Deep field | Near-black or deep surface, subtle tonal variation. | Documentation, product proof, quiet statements. |
| Signal field | A local accent around one current signal. | Hero artwork, active state, one callout. |
| Layered contour | Sparse bands or contour lines with a clear origin. | Taste, memory, pattern, and contextual relationships. |
| Nocturnal photograph | Dark observed photography with copy-safe space. | Website and editorial opening. |
| Paper field | Warm light surface with precise type and marks. | Typography, print, colour, and production references. |
| Artwork field | One approved or cleared artwork crop. | Player, Library, album-art treatment, editorial story. |
| Empty frame | No texture, no image, only a controlled surface. | Accessibility, tokens, dense reference, implementation. |

### Background rules

- Use one background family per spread.
- Let the surface disappear when it has completed its job.
- Avoid gradients that imply a feature or state without a label.
- Do not place dense interface text over grain, mist, or artwork detail.
- Prefer a quiet field over a weak decorative pattern.

## Photography direction

Photography should suggest attention and memory without staging an advertising lifestyle.

### Do

- Photograph one subject with a readable reason for being there.
- Use directional light and controlled shadow.
- Leave negative space for type and context.
- Show material wear, reflection, distance, or a return to an object.
- Use close crops when texture or attention matters.
- Keep the image specific enough to feel observed.

### Don’t

- Use generic headphones-on-a-desk stock imagery.
- Show crowds, parties, or status symbols to imply musical identity.
- Use posed “creative person” portraits with no listening relationship.
- Add fake album covers, fake provider UI, or unlicensed catalogue artwork.
- Lift shadows until every part of the image competes for attention.
- Use colour grading as a substitute for composition.

### Photography brief template

```text
Subject:
Musical relationship:
Focal point:
Light direction:
Copy-safe area:
Crop options:
Texture / depth:
Rights and provenance:
Alt text:
Status: Current / Fixture / Concept / Moodboard / TODO
```

## Album-art treatment

Album artwork is the most direct visual connection to the music object. Treat it with respect and hierarchy.

### Rules

- Preserve the artwork’s intended crop unless the application requires a documented crop.
- Give artwork a stable, readable frame before placing analytics or controls around it.
- Use background sampling only as a restrained environmental support, never as a replacement palette.
- Keep titles, artists, and album context adjacent and legible.
- Use artwork as evidence of the listening object, not as a generic texture.
- Label abstract fixtures and never attach them to real artists or albums without rights.

### Treatment matrix

| Context | Artwork treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Player | Clear square anchor, quiet surrounding controls, current state visible. |
| Library | Repeated artwork may create archive rhythm; titles remain readable. |
| Playlist Review | Artwork supports the collection story; findings remain separate and reviewable. |
| Discover | Artwork introduces the candidate; the reason for the recommendation remains visible. |
| Editorial page | One artwork may become a large hero, but the caption must explain its role. |
| Marketing | Use cleared or commissioned artwork and record provenance. |

## UI crops

UI crops should make hierarchy legible, not prove that many features exist.

### Crop scale

| Scale | Question answered | Typical content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full surface | Where am I and what is the product relationship? | Shell, route, Player, main surface. |
| Section crop | What is the primary decision? | Taste narrative, playlist review, Discover lane. |
| Component crop | How does one object behave? | Track row, connection card, empty state, Player control. |
| Detail crop | What is the visual rule? | Focus ring, reason label, confidence, artwork treatment. |

Use the largest crop that still preserves the context needed to understand the decision.

### UI crop annotations

Use no more than three callouts on a crop. Each callout should answer one of:

- What is the person looking at?
- Why is this information here?
- What can the person do next?

## Scale

Scale communicates importance and distance.

### Large

Use large scale for:

- a governing principle;
- one music object;
- one relationship or signal;
- a chapter transition;
- a single product decision.

Large does not mean loud. Leave space around the subject.

### Medium

Use medium scale for supporting context: a compact set of metrics, a relationship legend, a small artwork group, or a short rule set.

### Small

Use small scale for evidence, metadata, captions, status, implementation notes, and provenance. Small type must never carry the only meaning of a state.

### Scale test

View the page at thumbnail, reading, and print sizes. The same anchor should remain identifiable at all three scales.

## Page rhythm

Art direction should create a sequence, not a stack of equally weighted pages.

### Recommended rhythm

```text
hero → principle → product proof → diagram → reference → pause
```

Repeat the rhythm only when the chapter’s subject benefits from it. A visual pause should arrive every 8–15 pages in a long publication, but the pause can be a single sentence, a material field, a large crop, or an empty frame.

### Page questions

Before approving a spread, ask:

1. What should the reader feel first?
2. What should the reader understand second?
3. What should the reader be able to do with the information?
4. What can be removed without losing the decision?

## Editorial layouts

Use different layouts for different jobs while retaining the approved typography and spacing systems.

| Layout | When to use | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Statement | A principle deserves room. | One sentence, one field, one small anchor. |
| Image-led | Atmosphere must come before explanation. | Large image, short caption, minimal label. |
| Annotated | A visual rule needs operational detail. | Large object, three or fewer callouts, concise legend. |
| Comparison | Two states or decisions need contrast. | Equal visual weight, explicit labels, shared baseline. |
| Sequence | A workflow or change needs order. | Three to five steps with clear beginning and end. |
| Specimen | A system needs to be inspected. | Repeated controlled examples with labels. |
| Reference | Detail must remain available. | Table, rule, accessibility, implementation, TODO. |

Do not put a table on a hero spread merely because the source material contains a table.

## Review checklist

Before publishing an art-directed page:

- [ ] The visual has one primary job.
- [ ] The music object or musical relationship is identifiable.
- [ ] The visual status is marked as Current, Fixture, Concept, Moodboard, or TODO.
- [ ] The approved logo, typography, colour, and spacing systems are unchanged.
- [ ] The composition works with the logo removed.
- [ ] The visual does not imply unsupported product behaviour.
- [ ] The image, artwork, or fixture has provenance and usage rights.
- [ ] Important meaning is not encoded only through colour, texture, motion, or position.
- [ ] The crop works at intended desktop and narrow-width sizes.
- [ ] Alt text, captions, or a textual equivalent exists where needed.
- [ ] The page has enough quiet space to establish hierarchy.
- [ ] The page has a related source chapter and a clear next reference.
