# Resonant Design System
## VISUAL LANGUAGE

## Purpose

This chapter defines the visual language that exists around the logo, typography, colour, spacing, and interface components. It is the system that should make Resonant recognisable when the logo is hidden.

The visual language is not decoration. Every line, field, image, pattern, crop, transition, and material should communicate something about listening, memory, context, taste, connection, or resonance.

Use this chapter when creating product surfaces, editorial pages, mockups, marketing material, presentations, photography briefs, illustrations, motion concepts, and future art direction. Use the existing [Logo System](06-LOGO_SYSTEM.md), [Color System](07-COLOR_SYSTEM.md), [Typography](08-TYPOGRAPHY.md), and [Layout & Grid System](09-LAYOUT_GRID.md) as fixed Version 1 foundations.

## Visual philosophy

Resonant should feel like a quiet instrument for understanding music: attentive, spacious, specific, and alive to relationships. It should reward looking closely without demanding attention through spectacle.

### The emotional brief

Resonant should evoke:

- **Recognition:** the feeling of noticing a pattern in music that was already there.
- **Depth:** more meaning becoming available without the interface becoming heavier.
- **Warmth:** personal, human, and considered rather than clinical or performative.
- **Anticipation:** a possible next listen, framed with enough context to feel inviting.
- **Trust:** a clear distinction between what was observed, what was calculated, and what is suggested.
- **Stillness:** enough visual quiet for music and artwork to remain primary.

### The difference from ordinary music software

Ordinary music software often relies on endless feeds, equal-sized tiles, popularity signals, decorative equalizers, or constant motion. Resonant should feel different because it gives relationships a visual form.

| Ordinary pattern | Resonant direction |
| --- | --- |
| A feed of interchangeable recommendations | A considered sequence with a reason for each step |
| A dashboard that treats every metric as equally important | One musical subject, supported by the context needed to understand it |
| Reactive waves that imply energy without meaning | Signals, patterns, and flows that explain a musical relationship |
| Generic AI gradients and floating blobs | Structured fields that show provenance, confidence, or connection |
| Glossy surfaces everywhere | Material contrast used only when it clarifies hierarchy |
| A loud player competing with content | A persistent listening thread that stays available and quiet |

### The governing visual sentence

> Resonant makes invisible musical relationships feel visible, calm, and human.

Use this sentence as a filter. If a visual element cannot support a relationship, a decision, a memory, or the act of listening, remove it.

## The visual DNA

The Resonant visual language is built from five behaviours:

1. **Signal** — mark what matters now.
2. **Relationship** — show what connects to what.
3. **Memory** — let previous listening remain present without becoming a timeline wall.
4. **Distance** — show familiarity, uncertainty, or discovery range.
5. **Restraint** — leave enough quiet for meaning to register.

These behaviours can appear as a line, crop, field, annotation, node, contour, layer, or transition. The shape is secondary to the meaning.

```text
Music / artwork
       ↓
Observed signal → context → interpretation → choice
       ↑                                  ↓
     memory ←──────── listening thread ───┘
```

## Graphic language

### Signal

**Meaning:** the part of a surface that deserves attention now: a current track, an active route, a confidence value, a new finding, or a next action.

**Visual form:** a short line, point, pulse, highlight field, or restrained change in density. The signal should be precise and local, not a page-wide glow.

**Use it for:** active state, current listening, a meaningful change, a recommendation reason, or a single important annotation.

**Do:** make one signal legible before adding another.

**Do not:** turn every element into a signal. If everything glows, nothing is current.

### Wave

**Meaning:** change over time, rhythm, continuity, or a listening trace.

**Visual form:** a slow, imperfect curve with a clear beginning and end. The wave may be sparse, interrupted, layered, or partially hidden.

**Use it for:** listening history, motion studies, a transition between states, or an abstract representation of musical variation.

**Do:** let the wave carry a question: how did this change, where does it lead, or what repeats?

**Do not:** use a waveform as generic decoration, as a perpetual loading spinner, or as a promise of audio analysis that does not exist.

### Connection

**Meaning:** a relationship between two or more things: account and provider, track and playlist, listening history and taste signal, or recommendation and reason.

**Visual form:** thin paths, paired points, shared edges, relationship lines, or a quiet bridge between surfaces.

**Use it for:** trust boundaries, provenance, workflow steps, and contextual recommendations.

**Do:** distinguish a direct connection from a calculated relationship.

**Do not:** imply that a visual line means data is being transferred when the product has not verified that behaviour.

### Memory

**Meaning:** the residue of listening: repetition, return, archive, sequence, or a previous decision.

**Visual form:** offset layers, repeated frames, quiet echoes, stacked cards, worn edges, or a trace that remains after the main subject moves.

**Use it for:** Library, recently played context, playlist history, taste patterns, and editorial artwork that suggests return.

**Do:** show memory as specific and personal.

**Do not:** use nostalgia filters, retro props, or decorative timestamps without a real memory relationship.

### Pattern

**Meaning:** a recurring relationship in listening, not a score or personality verdict.

**Visual form:** a repeated rhythm with one useful deviation, a measured set of marks, or a restrained chart paired with language.

**Use it for:** Taste, playlist concentration, genre/era relationships, repetition, and change over time.

**Do:** provide a textual interpretation and provenance.

**Do not:** create a decorative pattern that looks analytical but communicates no question or conclusion.

### Constellation

**Meaning:** a set of related points where the relationship matters more than a single object.

**Visual form:** a sparse group of nodes with only the meaningful edges visible.

**Use it for:** artists, albums, genres, moods, eras, or discovery distance when the underlying relationship is known.

**Do:** keep the number of nodes small and explain the relationship.

**Do not:** use stars, planets, or a random night sky as an automatic “music” metaphor.

### Topography

**Meaning:** density, terrain, or gradual change across a field.

**Visual form:** contour lines, layered bands, gradients with direction, and areas of pressure or quiet.

**Use it for:** taste density, listening intensity, confidence ranges, or a visual explanation of distance from the familiar.

**Do:** make the field readable as a map of a real relationship.

**Do not:** add contour lines to a page just to make it feel technical.

### Flow

**Meaning:** a sequence of decisions or a movement through context.

**Visual form:** a restrained path, stepped sequence, or progressive reveal with clear endpoints.

**Use it for:** Spotify connection, search-to-result, analyse-to-review, and discovery journeys.

**Do:** show where a person can pause, correct, cancel, or return.

**Do not:** imply automation or irreversible mutation where the product requires review.

### Resonance

**Meaning:** two or more signals reinforcing one another without becoming identical.

**Visual form:** overlapping fields, paired curves, echoed shapes, or a shared alignment with a small difference.

**Use it for:** a track belonging to a playlist, an artist recurring across contexts, or a recommendation that is familiar but not repetitive.

**Do:** preserve separation between the signals.

**Do not:** use blur or glow to hide whether two things are actually related.

## Artwork system

Artwork is a communication layer, not a generic collection of attractive images. Each category has a job, an emotional temperature, and a boundary.

| Category | Purpose | Visual behaviour | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Editorial artwork | Open a chapter or establish the world of Resonant. | Quiet, cinematic, spacious, with one dominant idea. | Feature collages, generic lifestyle stock, or many competing messages. |
| Signal artwork | Make a current state or relationship visible. | Precise marks, local contrast, clear focal point. | Full-page noise that makes every element feel active. |
| Album artwork | Keep music and the listening object primary. | Respect the artwork’s crop, colour, and hierarchy. | Treating album art as a background texture behind unrelated copy. |
| Photography | Give the brand a specific, observed human atmosphere. | Honest light, tactile detail, considered negative space. | Headphone clichés, staged productivity, or anonymous “music lifestyle” scenes. |
| Product renders | Explain how philosophy becomes a surface. | One large proof, one short rationale, labelled concept status. | Inventing shipped screens or filling a page with tiny UI. |
| Abstract forms | Translate relationships that cannot be photographed. | Structured fields, lines, nodes, layers, and controlled variation. | Generic AI blobs, random liquid chrome, or unmotivated 3D objects. |
| Generative visuals | Explore variation while preserving a defined visual grammar. | Parameterised signal, pattern, density, and distance. | Unbounded novelty, visual effects without a data question, or unreviewed outputs. |
| Illustrations | Explain a system, boundary, or workflow. | Geometric, labelled, sparse, and diagrammatic. | Mascots, decorative scenes, or illustrations that imply unsupported features. |

### Artwork hierarchy

When several artwork categories appear together, use this order:

1. **Music object** — album artwork, track identity, or listening state.
2. **Human context** — memory, taste, history, or a reason to care.
3. **System explanation** — analytics, AI, connection, or workflow.
4. **Atmosphere** — texture, photography, or abstract field.

Atmosphere may support the first three layers, but must never outrank the music object.

## Photography

Photography should feel observed rather than staged. It should suggest the attention of listening without showing a generic person performing “music taste”.

### Lighting

- Prefer directional, low-key light with a readable falloff.
- Use a small area of brightness as a signal, not a full-frame wash.
- Allow darkness to remain dark; do not lift every shadow for information that is not needed.
- Use reflections, haze, and bloom only when they describe atmosphere or depth.
- Keep skin, materials, and artwork believable when people or physical objects appear.

### Composition

- Give the subject one clear job: establish place, memory, material, or listening attention.
- Use negative space for editorial type and context.
- Prefer asymmetry with a stable anchor over perfectly centred lifestyle framing.
- Let a crop feel intentional: close enough to notice, wide enough to place.
- Keep high-frequency detail away from critical copy and controls.

### Subjects and locations

Appropriate subjects include quiet rooms, record shelves, cables, desks after use, worn listening objects, studio materials, architectural thresholds, night surfaces, reflections, and close views of hands or objects when they communicate attention.

Appropriate locations include calm interiors, small listening spaces, studios, archival environments, minimal architecture, corridors, windows at night, and places where sound could plausibly be remembered.

Avoid staged parties, anonymous nightlife, luxury interiors, generic headphones-on-a-desk scenes, exaggerated performance poses, and imagery that suggests social status rather than personal listening.

### Cropping, depth, and grading

- Crop around the musical or material detail, not around a decorative prop.
- Use foreground, subject, and background only when the layers explain attention or distance.
- Grade toward the approved dark, warm, restrained palette without recolouring the approved system.
- Keep highlights controlled and shadows useful.
- Do not use a filter to manufacture nostalgia.

## Abstract artwork

The wave concept can become a strong Resonant system if it is treated as a language for relationships rather than an effect.

### The abstract grammar

Build an abstract visual from no more than three primary ingredients:

1. **A field** — the quiet space in which a relationship exists.
2. **A signal** — the thing that changes, repeats, or deserves attention.
3. **A relationship** — the line, echo, distance, or overlap that gives the signal meaning.

```text
Field → signal → relationship → interpretation
quiet     mark       path / echo       reason
```

### Translating Resonant concepts

| Concept | Possible visual translation | Required meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Music | A changing line, pulse, or layered rhythm. | Time, variation, or listening movement. |
| Patterns | Repeated marks with one meaningful change. | Recurrence, not decoration. |
| Memory | Offset echoes or stacked traces. | Return, archive, or previous context. |
| Taste | A field with clusters and distances. | A pattern of preference, not a score. |
| Connections | Sparse nodes and intentional paths. | A known relationship or boundary. |
| Recommendations | A path that moves from familiar to adjacent. | Distance with a reason. |
| Context | A signal shown with its surrounding evidence. | Why this matters here. |

### How to avoid generic AI blobs

- Start from a named relationship before choosing a form.
- Limit the number of degrees of freedom: line count, node count, density, and movement should be intentional.
- Keep a visible origin or anchor so the viewer can understand the composition.
- Prefer edge, contour, cut, and overlap to soft amorphous volume.
- Pair an abstract visual with a short explanation when the meaning is not self-evident.
- Never imply that a generated shape is a literal representation of a person’s taste.

## Shapes and geometry

Resonant may repeat a small family of shapes so the visual language feels authored:

| Shape | Role | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rounded rectangle | A container for a decision, object, or state. | Use when the boundary matters; do not make every surface a card. |
| Diagonal cut | Direction, separation, or an opening between forms. | May echo the logo’s logic in surrounding graphics, but never modify the logo. |
| Signal curve | Time, continuity, and change. | Keep endpoints and direction legible. |
| Circle or point | A node, current state, or point of attention. | Use sparingly; do not create a starfield. |
| Negative space | Separation, listening room, and hierarchy. | Treat empty space as an active component. |
| Contour band | Density, range, or gradual distance. | Use only when the field represents a real relationship. |

The logo may inspire a family resemblance through rounded terminals, controlled diagonals, and deliberate negative space. It must never be traced, fragmented, tiled, or reconstructed into decorative patterns.

## Texture and material

Texture should communicate material, age, atmosphere, or physicality. It should not be a permanent layer over every page.

| Material | Version 1 direction | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paper | Quiet, warm, and lightly tactile. | Editorial pages, print references, typography and colour specimens. |
| Film grain | Very subtle and low contrast. | Photography and atmosphere when it prevents digital sterility. |
| Glass | Rare and functional. | Only where transparency explains layering or separation; not as a default card style. |
| Noise | Minimal and controlled. | Softening a large field or supporting a generative study. |
| Mist | Atmospheric, directional, and sparse. | Photography or a visual pause; never behind dense UI text. |
| Light bloom | Local and restrained. | A signal or depth cue, never a page-wide glow. |
| None | The default for interface rules and precise diagrams. | Tables, documentation, accessibility, tokens, and implementation pages. |

### Texture rules

- Texture must survive print and remain accessible when removed.
- Do not use noise to disguise weak composition.
- Do not layer paper, grain, glass, mist, bloom, and glow together.
- Keep texture out of focus rings, labels, controls, and small type.
- If a texture is removed and the meaning disappears, the texture was doing too much.

## Composition

Resonant compositions should feel balanced but not symmetrical by default. Use asymmetry to make the relationship between subject and context visible.

### The composition formula

```text
one anchor + one context layer + one quiet field
```

- **Anchor:** the music object, statement, mark, or current signal.
- **Context layer:** annotation, connection, memory, evidence, or surrounding artwork.
- **Quiet field:** negative space, restrained surface, or controlled atmosphere.

### Composition rules

- Let the primary subject arrive before the explanation.
- Use a large crop or a single statement when the idea is emotional or foundational.
- Use a diagram when the relationship is sequential or conditional.
- Use a grid when comparison is the point.
- Use asymmetry when it clarifies distance or emphasis; do not use it as random disruption.
- Keep one dominant reading path per spread.
- Use the approved spacing and layout tokens; visual language must not create a parallel spacing system.

## Depth

Depth should make relationships easier to read, not make Resonant look like a 3D product showcase.

### Depth model

Use up to three visual layers:

1. **Field:** background, surface, or atmospheric context.
2. **Subject:** artwork, signal, component, or primary statement.
3. **Evidence:** annotation, connection, caption, or supporting trace.

Overlap is useful when it shows one thing affecting or sitting near another. Avoid floating objects without an explanation of their relationship.

### Depth rules

- Prefer tonal separation, crop, and overlap over heavy shadows.
- Keep interface layers legible and reversible.
- Do not make the background disappear behind constant blur.
- Use elevation only where the product hierarchy requires it; see [Design Tokens](26-DESIGN_TOKENS.md).
- Treat artwork and UI as neighbouring layers, not as one blended surface.

## Iconography

Resonant iconography should feel minimal, technical, and human at the same time: outlined, rounded, quiet, and optically precise.

The current Lucide React direction remains the authority. Use the 24×24, 2 px, rounded-cap basis until the icon inventory is formally audited.

### Icon character

- Outlined before filled.
- Rounded caps and joins before sharp mechanical corners.
- One icon per action.
- Consistent optical weight at neighbouring sizes.
- Familiar metaphor before abstract symbol.
- Labels beside unfamiliar actions.

Icons should describe an action or state, not decorate an empty area. Do not use a waveform as a generic loading icon, mix unrelated icon families, or turn the logo geometry into a general icon set.

See [Iconography](17-ICONOGRAPHY.md) and [Accessibility](18-ACCESSIBILITY.md) for implementation and assistive-technology rules.

## Motion language

Motion should feel like context becoming available, not like a performance layered on top of music.

### Motion philosophy

- **Arrive:** a new context appears with enough movement to show where it came from.
- **Settle:** the new state becomes quiet and readable.
- **Breathe:** a waiting state may have a restrained rhythm, but it must never impersonate audio.
- **Connect:** related surfaces may move along a path or preserve a shared anchor.
- **Discover:** a new possibility should enter from the context that makes it relevant.
- **Evolve:** patterns may change gradually when the underlying data changes.
- **Yield:** reduced motion removes atmosphere, not information.

Do not design the interface to pulse with the beat, fill the screen with parallax, or animate every card independently. Motion must preserve focus, state, cancellation, and accessibility.

See [Motion](24-MOTION.md), [Interaction Patterns](25-INTERACTION_PATTERNS.md), and [Loading, Empty, and Error States](27-LOADING_ERROR_STATES.md).

## UI and artwork relationship

The hierarchy remains:

```text
Music → artwork → interface → explanation → atmosphere
```

- **Music** is the reason the surface exists.
- **Artwork** identifies and emotionally anchors the listening object.
- **Interface** makes the next action possible.
- **Explanation** gives analytics, AI, or recommendation context.
- **Atmosphere** creates continuity but never obscures the first four layers.

### Coexistence rules

- Give album artwork its own readable area before adding analysis around it.
- Place UI controls on quiet surfaces or protect them with contrast-safe fields.
- Keep abstract artwork behind the relationship it explains, not in front of it.
- Use photography to establish attention or memory, not to replace product evidence.
- Label concept UI clearly; do not let atmospheric imagery make a future feature look shipped.
- When artwork and data compete, reduce the data decoration first.

## Brand recognition without the logo

Someone should be able to recognise Resonant from a page, crop, or motion fragment with the logo removed. Use this checklist:

- Music is the primary subject, never a background excuse.
- One signal is given priority over a field of equal cards.
- Relationships are visible through paths, echoes, clusters, or annotations.
- Discovery has distance and a reason, not only a ranking.
- Data is framed as context, estimate, or evidence—not as a verdict.
- The composition has a quiet field and a clear anchor.
- The visual grammar uses restrained curves, nodes, cuts, layers, and negative space.
- Texture is selective and meaningful.
- UI remains calm enough for the music to lead.
- Motion reveals context and yields to the listener.
- Accessibility is visible in hierarchy, labels, focus, and recovery.
- The result feels personal and observant, never performative or gamified.

### Recognition test

Remove the logo, product name, and brand copy from a visual. Ask:

1. Is the musical object still the reason for the composition?
2. Can I see what is connected, changing, remembered, or recommended?
3. Is there one clear signal and enough quiet around it?
4. Does the visual avoid generic entertainment, dashboard, and AI tropes?
5. Would the same grammar work across a UI crop, editorial page, and physical application?

If the answer is no, the visual needs a stronger relationship or less decoration.

## Resonant should never become

| Direction | Why it is rejected |
| --- | --- |
| Cyberpunk | It makes complexity feel aggressive and turns music into a tech spectacle. |
| Neon gaming | It treats attention as a reward loop instead of respecting listening. |
| Crypto dashboard | It suggests speculation, status, and financial performance rather than personal context. |
| Generic SaaS | It flattens music into feature cards and removes the emotional reason to return. |
| Glass overload | It makes every layer translucent, weakens hierarchy, and creates contrast debt. |
| Luxury fashion | It can make the product feel exclusive, posed, and status-driven. |
| Warm gold branding | Gold is an approved product signal in restrained contexts, not the complete identity. Do not let it replace Resonant’s broader visual language. |
| Apple clone | Minimalism without Resonant’s musical relationships becomes generic. |
| Spotify clone | Catalogue-first layouts and provider-coded patterns weaken Resonant’s context-first identity. |
| Generic AI blob | Soft gradients without evidence imply intelligence without explaining it. |
| Decorative equalizer | It references music without communicating listening, memory, or context. |
| Endless recommendation feed | It turns discovery into consumption and removes the possibility of a considered pause. |

## Production guidance

### Asset provenance

Every visual asset needs:

- source or creator;
- usage rights and licence status;
- intended channel;
- crop and contrast notes;
- alt text or caption where applicable;
- whether it is current product evidence, approved fixture, concept, or moodboard reference.

### Responsive behaviour

Visual language must survive the same responsive constraints as the product:

- keep the anchor visible at narrow widths;
- collapse relationships into a readable sequence rather than a compressed network;
- preserve captions, labels, and contrast when artwork crops change;
- never hide the Player, connection state, or recovery path beneath atmosphere;
- use the mobile rules in [Mobile](29-MOBILE.md) only as future direction until the product scope is verified.

### Accessibility

Visual language is not exempt from accessibility. Provide text alternatives for charts, patterns, and abstract relationships. Do not encode current state, confidence, discovery distance, or connection only through colour, glow, motion, texture, or position. Support keyboard focus, reduced motion, 200% zoom, and clear reading order.

### Implementation notes

- Keep the existing logo, typography, colour, and spacing tokens as the underlying system.
- Add visual language as semantic roles such as `signal`, `relationship`, `memory`, `distance`, and `quiet-field`, not as arbitrary decoration classes.
- Use the product repository as the authority for current screens and behaviour.
- Use fixture data only when clearly labelled; never use abstract artwork to imply real user data.
- Prefer a small number of authored primitives over a large library of unrelated effects.
- Test the system with the logo removed, the colour reduced to monochrome, and the texture disabled.
